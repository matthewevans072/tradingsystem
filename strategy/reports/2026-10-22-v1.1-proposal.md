## Why cb-1.0.0 almost never enters

Write the frozen rules in one picture. The terms:

- `R` is the ceiling, `H` the box height, `A` the ATR.
- `c` is the breakout close and `ℓ` the breakout candle's low.
- `d = c − R` is how far the close is above the ceiling.
- `b = c − ℓ` is the distance from the close down to the candle's low.

The rules set three things:

- **Stop:** `ℓ − 0.1A`.
- **Target:** `R + H`.
- **Entry:** must keep reward ≥ 2 × risk.

Ignoring costs, an entry at the breakout close passes the 2:1 limit only if:

```
d + 2b + 0.2A  ≤  H        (and H ≤ 3A by the box rule)
```

A candle that breaks out from inside the box has its low at or below the
ceiling, so `b ≥ d`. A typical 15-minute breakout candle spans most of an ATR.
The left side then uses up most or all of a box that is at most 3 ATR tall.
The next bar's open is usually higher still.

So the three rules conflict with each other:

- The **stop** is sized by the breakout candle.
- The **target** is sized by the box.
- The **2:1 limit** demands that the box-sized reward be twice the
  candle-sized risk.

That is why the maximum entry sat below the breakout close in 84 of 85
skipped orders. It is a property of the rule logic, not of a setting that
scored badly.

The fix therefore has to change one of the two sides of that inequality:

- **Where risk is measured from** (the stop): candidate A.
- **What the reward is measured against** (the target): candidate B.

The third route is to lower the 2:1 minimum to some other number. That would
mean choosing a value, and the only basis for choosing it now would be the
results already seen. It is not proposed.

## Candidate A — cb-1.1.0-A: stop at the broken ceiling

**Change (one rule):** stop = **ceiling − 0.1 × ATR**, instead of the breakout
candle's low − 0.1 × ATR. Everything else is unchanged:

- the box rules;
- volume above 1.5×;
- entry at the next open;
- the 0.5 ATR gap limit and the 2:1 limit;
- target = ceiling + box height;
- the costs and the account.

**Logic.** A breakout says price has moved above a level that held it back.
The thesis is wrong once price trades back below that level. The breakout
candle's low has no such meaning: it measures how far that candle travelled,
not where the idea fails.

RULES.md already calls the candle-low stop "the chosen interpretation" of an
ambiguous source. Candidate A re-reads that one ambiguous point. It does not
change anything the brief stated outright.

With this stop, the 2:1 condition at the close becomes:

```
d ≤ (H − 0.2A) / 3
```

The entry passes when the close is within about a third of the box height
above the ceiling. That no longer depends on how long the breakout candle is.

**How it differs from the intrabar-limit stress variant.** The variant
traded more but had negative mean R:

| | Variant | Candidate A |
|---|---|---|
| How it fills | A resting limit that fills only if the next bar trades down to the maximum entry | Fills only at the next bar's open, as in cb-1.0.0 |
| Which entries it adds | Ones where price fell back after the breakout candle: buying weakness | Opens within the reach of the new limit: buying while the breakout holds |
| A dip back below the ceiling | Is the variant's way to get filled | Is A's stop-out |

So A is not that variant.

**Risks, known now:**
- Stops close to the ceiling will be hit by ordinary retests of the broken
  level. On 15-minute bars that may happen often, making the win rate lower.
- Risk per share becomes small when `d` is small. The existing
  cost-to-risk filter rejects trades where costs exceed 20% of risk, so many
  small-risk signals may be rejected for costs instead. No new minimum-stop
  value is proposed: `stop.minStopAtr` stays 0.
- Smaller risk per share means more shares for the same 0.25% risk. Gaps
  through the stop overnight in swing mode can then cost more than 1 R.

## Candidate B — cb-1.1.0-B: target from risk

**Change (one rule):** target = **entry + 2 × risk**, set at the simulated
fill from the actual effective entry. Equivalently, the reward-to-risk is
exactly 2:1 at every fill. Because 2:1 then holds by construction, the
reward-to-risk entry limit drops away. The maximum entry becomes the 0.5 ATR
gap limit alone. Everything else is unchanged:

- the stop (breakout candle low − 0.1 ATR);
- the box rules, volume and entry timing;
- the costs and the account.

The "2" is the existing `minRewardRisk`, not a new number.

**Logic.** It removes the mismatch between a candle-sized risk and a
box-sized reward by sizing the reward from the risk. The target is set from
the fill price, which is known at the open, so it uses no future data.

**How it differs from the intrabar-limit stress variant:**

| | Variant | Candidate B |
|---|---|---|
| How it fills | Only on a dip inside the next bar, to the old cap | Only at the next open |
| Which entries it adds | Opens below the old cap reached by a dip | Opens above the old cap, up to 0.5 ATR above the breakout close: continuation, not weakness |

So B is not that variant either.

**Costs of this change:**
- **It departs from the brief.** The brief stated "target = ceiling + box
  height" outright. B replaces it.
- **Some targets will be far away.** A long breakout candle gives a large risk
  and a target twice as far. Intraday, many of these trades may end at the
  15:45 flatten rather than at the target.

## Considered and dropped

- **Buying the retest.** This means a resting buy limit at or near the
  ceiling for several bars after the breakout. It is essentially the
  intrabar-limit stress variant spread over more bars: it gets filled
  precisely when price falls back after the breakout. **Dropped.**
- **A lower minimum reward-to-risk** (such as 1.5:1) or a larger target
  multiple. Either would mean choosing a number with only seen results to
  choose by. **Dropped.**
- **Removing the 2:1 limit without replacing it.** It would allow entries with
  reward below risk, and there is no rule-logic reason for that. **Dropped.**

## What counts as failure (decided now, before any result)

These apply to whichever single candidate is chosen, in each mode that is
taken forward. acceptance-v1 is not changed.

**1. Implementation (before any run).**
- The engine change passes the existing look-ahead tests (prefix invariance,
  snapshot round-trip and determinism), plus new tests for the changed rule.
- `ENGINE-SPEC.md`, `RULES.md` and a new `configs/cb-1.1.0.json` agree with
  the code.
- A failure here is a bug to fix, not a strategy failure. It does not use up
  anything.

**2. In-sample screen on development and validation.** These periods are
seen, so the screen can only rule out. Any of the following means **failed:
the holdout is not run and cb-1.0.0 is closed**:

| Code | Failure condition | Why |
|---|---|---|
| S1 | Fewer than **60** closed validation trades | B1 could not pass |
| S2 | Validation mean R **≤ +0.10** | B2 could not pass |
| S3 | Development mean R **≤ 0** | |
| S4 | Fewer than **70%** of the 12 validation neighbours have mean R > 0 | B8 |
| S5 | Walk-forward out-of-sample mean R **≤ 0** | B10 |
| S6 | Mechanism check: fewer than **half** of the orders placed in development and validation are filled | The change did not fix the entry problem it was made for |

Passing the screen is **not** evidence of an edge. It only means the holdout
may be used.

**3. Holdout: one run, the only real test.** acceptance-v1 B1–B7 and B9 are
judged on the holdout, unchanged. Any failure means **not eligible, and
cb-1.0.0 is closed with no v1.2**. "Inconclusive", for example too few
holdout trades, also ends the line. There is no second holdout run.

**4. After a pass.** Even "eligible for review" is not permission to trade. A
forward test with F1–F4 would still be required, and it needs a paid
real-time feed.
