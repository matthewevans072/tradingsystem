## 1. What is different from campaigns 1 and 2

Campaign 2 searched the range-trend engine's parameters around four bases (a 60-close range with zones, breakouts and pullbacks from it). Campaign 3 tests **mechanisms the range-trend engine cannot express**: channel breakouts without a qualified range, breakouts after volatility compression, long-horizon new highs with a moving-average exit, and three mean-reversion entries (consecutive down closes, a band in standard deviations, a gap down) with a long-term trend filter. Each family has a **simple baseline** so that each mechanism is read against the plainest version of its own idea, and every run is read against **matched buy-and-hold benchmarks**.

**The statistical adjustment.** The campaign's per-experiment null test (random timing, p ≤ 0.05) is a *screen*, not a proof: under the hypothesis that timing adds nothing, 5% of experiments pass it by chance, so a campaign of n experiments expects 0.05 × n chance passes (campaign 2: 4.7 expected, 0 observed; its smallest p was 0.218). A family-wise correction within a campaign is the honest bar for calling a single pass evidence. **The arithmetic for campaign 2, corrected:** Bonferroni over its 94 experiments is 0.05 / 94 = 0.000532; with 2,000 resamples the smallest attainable p is 1 / 2,001 = 0.000500, so the bar was attainable, but only by a result that no resample matched, and the p-values were graded in steps of 0.0005, too coarse to rank anything near the bar. Across programmes a single correction is not meaningful, because they test different hypotheses with different statistics; what accumulates across them is **selection bias on the development window**: any rule kept because it looked best on these bars overstates what it would do on new bars, and the overstatement grows with the number of rule-sets tried (105 so far). The accounting adopted for campaign 3, **retaining every acceptance rule of CAMPAIGN-1.md section 6** and adding to the report:
1. **The null, exactly.** For a completed experiment, the items are its completed trades and its positions open at the end. Item j has stock s_j, shares q_j and holding length h_j = x_j − e_j sessions, where e_j is the index (in the stock's own session list) of the entry fill and x_j of the exit fill (h_j ≥ 1); an open position has x_j = the last session and exits at that close. Its eligible decision set D_j is every index i with i ≥ the period's first session, i + 1 + h_j ≤ the last session, and bars for s_j at both i + 1 and i + 1 + h_j; the set is enumerated once per (stock, h) and a draw is uniform over it (no rejection, no skipped placements). Resample r moves every item to its own draw: entry price O(i + 1) × (1 + k(s_j, i + 1)), exit price O(i + 1 + h_j) × (1 − k(s_j, i + 1 + h_j)) for a completed trade and C(i + 1 + h_j) for an open position, k = (half-spread + slippage) as in section 2; T_r = Σ_j q_j × (exit − entry). The observed statistic T_obs is the same sum over the run's recorded fill prices (spread and slippage included, fees and dividends excluded). **R = 20,000 resamples**, one mulberry32 stream seeded 20261105 for every experiment (common random numbers), consumed in item order, so every run is reproducible. **p = (1 + #{r : T_r ≥ T_obs}) / (1 + R)**; the smallest attainable p is 1 / 20,001 = 0.00005 and the grading step is 0.00005. The per-experiment threshold stays p ≤ 0.05.
2. **The Holm family.** m = 24, the planned configurations, fixed before any run; a failed, incomplete or trade-less experiment does not shrink m and is treated as not passing (its p is reported as "none"). The family's p-values are ordered p(1) ≤ ... ≤ p(m'), m' ≤ m; p(i) is Holm-significant when p(j) ≤ 0.05 / (m − j + 1) for every j ≤ i. Every report states the expected chance passes (0.05 × m') beside the observed passes, and marks each result that clears the target as Holm-significant or not, labelled "within-campaign". The shortlist rule itself is unchanged (the per-experiment screen); Holm is reported, not enforced, because the retained rules are not lowered or raised here.
3. every campaign document and report records the cumulative count of runs and distinct rule-sets on this data hash before it started (campaign 3 starts at 127 runs and 105 rule-sets), and a **defect replacement** (section 8) is counted as a further run on the same rule-set;
4. a shortlisted result is reported as "shortlisted on the development window after N rule-sets", never as evidence. **The evaluation window 2022-01-03 to 2023-12-29 and its prior exposure:** no programme's code has run on the ten stocks' *daily* bars in that window; but the Strategy Lab's consolidation-breakout runs 1 to 12 (2026-10-22) used MU's 15-minute bars over 2021-07-01 to 2024-12-31 (its development and validation windows), which overlap that window for MU, and its 2025-01-01 to 2026-06-30 holdout window for MU was never run; HXR's pre-registration disclosed this and that the assistant's training data and the owner's own memory cover these stocks' history to mid-2026, so no historical window is truly unseen, and only a prospective account is clean. The range-trend protocol opens the window once for one frozen candidate on the owner's separate message; no development p-value replaces that, and that run would itself carry the MU exposure above.
5. **What the null is, and is not.** The random-timing null is a *trade-level diagnostic*: it asks whether the run's entry timing, trade by trade, beat random placement of the same stock, shares and holding length. It does not enforce portfolio feasibility: the random placements ignore cash, the position and risk caps, overlapping positions in one stock and same-day sharing, so a resample's P/L need not be achievable by the portfolio rules. It therefore says nothing about sizing or exposure (the exposure-matched hold does), and a pass is evidence about timing only.
6. **Raw and adjusted p, both visible.** Every result shows its raw p (the per-experiment screen) and its within-campaign Holm-adjusted p. A result that clears the target and passes the shortlist conditions with its raw p is an **unadjusted shortlist pass** (the retained rule); it is labelled **Holm-significant** only if its adjusted p is at or below 0.05. The two labels are shown apart in the dashboard, the report and the notifications, and neither is evidence of anything beyond the development window.

## 4. Benchmarks and baselines

- **The fixed positive baseline** for the 10% target: +US$1,757.90 (campaign 2's baseline), re-run first.
- **Family baselines:** B0 and M0 (section 5), the plainest version of each idea, run as experiments and reported beside every mechanism of their family (the mechanism's net minus its family baseline's net, and the exposure attribution between them).
- **Exposure attribution** (retained): the change in the exposure-matched hold's P/L between a run and the baseline is the exposure part; the remainder is the decision (selection or timing) part; the decision part must be positive for a shortlist.

None of these mechanisms has a profit-taking exit; every exit above is a signal exit or a time exit, decided at a close and filled at the next open. The sizing reference of each mechanism is separate (section 5a).

### 5a. The sizing reference S of every mechanism (fixed at the entry decision; never an exit)

S is used once, to size the order: planned risk = shares × (fill price − S), capped at 1% of the account value at the decision close; S is never sent as an order and never triggers an exit. It must lie strictly below the entry close, which the last column proves. Exits are the signal and time exits of section 5 only, so a trade's realised loss can be larger than its planned risk (an exit needs a close beyond the signal level and fills at the next open, and a time exit sells wherever the price is) or smaller; the share of trades whose loss exceeded the planned risk is reported for every run (measured). Where the 1% rule and the 20% position cap both apply, the cap binds whenever S is within 5% of the fill (1% ÷ 20%), as in campaign 2.

| Mechanism | S at the decision close t | Distance below the close | Why S < C(t) always | Relation to the exits |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| B0, B1 | LL(20)(t−1), frozen | C(t) − LL(20) | C(t) > HH(20) ≥ LL(20) | the signal exit is the *rolling* LL(20), which can rise above the frozen S; S is not the exit |
| B2 | C(t) − 3 × m(t−1), frozen | 3 × m | m > 0 (a zero m gives no signal) | the give-back exit trails the highest close by the same 3 × m, so a losing trade's first possible exit close is below S by at least one bar's move; the exit still fills at the next open |
| B3 | SMA(s)(t−1), frozen | C(t) − SMA(s) | C(t) > HH(h) ≥ HH(s) ≥ SMA(s) for every pinned pair, since h ≥ s | the signal exit is the *rolling* SMA(s); S is not the exit |
| M0, M1, M2, M3 | C(t) − 2 × m(t−1), frozen (one rule for the family) | 2 × m | m > 0 | the exits are an up-close, a mean, a recovered close or a time exit, none of which is S; a band or gap level was considered for M2 and M3 and rejected because it can sit above the entry close (a close more than k + 1 deviations below its mean) or above the fill after a gap |

**Next-open gaps and sizing.** The order is sized at the decision close with C(t) as the provisional price; at the fill F (the next open plus spread and slippage) every limit is applied again at F: planned risk = shares × (F − S) ≤ 1% of the decision-close account value, the 20% cap at F, the cash share including costs. A gap up widens F − S, so the share count is reduced (never raised); a gap down to F ≤ S makes the entry **rejected** and recorded (the planned risk would be undefined), exactly as campaign 2 recorded `stop_above_fill`; a gap that leaves no whole share cancels the order. Rejections and reductions are reported per run and per stock.

No combinations in this campaign; no parameter is tuned after a result is read; the three "without the filter" variants are the only paired comparisons (the filter's own effect). A mechanism that produces fewer than 30 trades is reported as such and cannot clear the target.

## 6. Acceptance (retained from CAMPAIGN-1.md section 6, with the accounting of section 3)

A result clears the target when its net profit is at least 10% above +US$1,757.90, its maximum drawdown is at most 15%, it has at least 30 completed trades, the accounting check passes and the data-quality check passed. It is an **unadjusted shortlist pass** when it also passes the best-5% stress (net with the top ceil(5% n) outcomes removed still at least the baseline's), the random-timing null with its raw p (20,000 resamples, seed 20261105, p ≤ 0.05), the 40% single-stock gross-gain gate and the positive decision gain. It is additionally **Holm-significant (within-campaign)** when its Holm-adjusted p over the family of 24 is at or below 0.05; that label is reported beside the shortlist, never merged with it. The report adds the expected chance passes (0.05 × the number of completed experiments with a p), the raw and adjusted p of every result, and the cumulative counts. Each mechanism is also compared with its family baseline (B0 or M0): the difference in net profit and its exposure and decision parts. Notifications as before, labelled development results. Nothing is lowered later; a change is campaign 4.

## 7. A 17-stock or ETF universe: not proposed here, and why

Verified on 2026-11-05 (read-only, counts and dates): `hxr-sim.sqlite` holds daily bars for basket A from 2016-01-04, for HOOD from 2021-07-29, and for MUU and NBIS from later dates; it holds no ETF. The Strategy Lab's 17 symbols (AMD, CAT, FCX, GLD, HD, IWM, JNJ, JPM, MSFT, MU and the rest) exist only as 15-minute bars from 2021-07-01, so **no daily history for them covers 2017-10-02 to 2021-12-31**, and no ETF history exists at all. A universe experiment is therefore not feasible with the data on disk; it would need a separate download on the owner's authorization, its own data version and hashes, its own equal-weight index and exposure-matched holds, and its own campaign document with a separate identifier, so that a universe difference is never attributed to strategy logic. Not part of this batch.

## 8. Implementation after approval (not before)

A new deterministic engine module for these mechanisms (one position per stock, the portfolio rules of section 2 shared with campaign 2 by reusing its sizing and cost code where possible), snapshot and prefix-invariance tests on synthetic data, the runner's queue, evaluation and dashboard reused; `CODE_REVISION` bumped; the campaign created only from this file once frozen. The runner applies the Holm step-down over the family of 24 when the campaign completes and stores both p-values with each result; the dashboard shows raw p and Holm p in separate columns. Budget: **24 planned configurations, and 24 only.** The campaign cap of 30 leaves six slots that are **defect replacements only**: a planned configuration whose result is invalidated by a verified defect (the defect, how it was verified and the correction recorded as non-empty text in EXPERIMENT-LOG.md, the broken result kept, the replacement counted as a further run on the same rule-set in the cumulative history) may be re-run once in one of them; they are never used for a new or discretionary variant, and unused slots stay unused. 2 hours of worker time, one worker, the same stop rules. The evaluation window stays closed; no new broad review cycle; no live trading.
